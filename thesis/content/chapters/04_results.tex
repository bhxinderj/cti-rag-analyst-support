\label{sec:results-generation}

Table~\ref{tab:generation-results} reports the RAGAS scores of all
configurations. Every local value is a three-run mean with standard
deviation (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{tables/ragas_final_v1}}
    \caption{RAGAS results per pipeline (28 queries, judge gpt-4o-mini).
             Rows correspond, top to bottom, to configurations V4, V3,
             V1, V2, V0, and V5 of Table~\ref{tab:configurations}. The

    \label{tab:generation-results}

The templated pipeline (V4) reaches the best local values in answer
relevancy (0.739), answer correctness (0.518), and context recall
(0.610), and it is the only configuration that never abstains; the
legacy prompt abstains on 7 of 28 queries. Its faithfulness (0.662)
stays close to the legacy configuration (0.689).

Two values require care when reading the table. The highest faithfulness
belongs to the BM25-only configuration (0.797); as noted in the caption,
this reflects its abstention behavior, not better grounding: the
configuration declines the queries it cannot support and answers the
remaining ones close to the retrieved text. The relevancy of the hosted
ablation (0.594) carries a measurement artifact\footnote{The relevancy
filter that nulls non-committal answers is calibrated on the phrasing of
the local 8B model. The hosted model formulates gap statements
differently and is nulled on 5--8 of 28 queries per run. The filter
stayed frozen for consistency across configurations.} and is discussed with the ablation in
Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation}.

The non-retrieval baseline (V0) reads well in isolation: its answer
relevancy (0.685) and answer correctness (0.502) sit at the level of the
grounded configurations. Faithfulness and the context metrics are
undefined without retrieval; what the fluent surface hides becomes
visible in the rubric results of
Section~\ref{sec:representative-outputs}. The vector-only configuration
(V2) is the weakest across all metrics;
Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation} traces this to its failure on exact
CTI identifiers.

\section{Baseline comparison and ablation}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

The configuration matrix isolates three effects: retrieval versus
parametric knowledge (V0 against the grounded configurations), the
retrieval strategy (V1--V3), and the generation mode (V3 against V4).
The hosted ablation (V5) adds the deployment dimension. All numbers
refer to Tables~\ref{tab:generation-results}
and~\ref{tab:rubric-results}.

\paragraph{Retrieval versus parametric knowledge.} On this query set,
retrieval buys verifiability rather than raw correctness. The baseline
answers prominent, well-documented CVEs from parametric knowledge at a
correctness level comparable to the grounded configurations (0.502
versus 0.518 for V4); the query set names prominent CVEs and therefore
favors the baseline systematically, which
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} records as a limitation. The rubric
separates the configurations where RAGAS does not: the baseline's
traceability of 1.23 against 2.56 for V4 means that an analyst cannot
verify the baseline's claims --- and unverifiable output cannot be acted
on in CTI work (B3, C1).

\paragraph{Retrieval strategy.} Each single-strategy retriever fails in
a characteristic way. The vector-only configuration collapses on exact
CTI identifiers: it abstains on 14--15 of 28 queries per run and scores
lowest on every metric, because embedding similarity does not reliably
surface passages for identifier queries such as specific CVE numbers.
The BM25-only configuration holds identifier queries but loses semantic
coverage; its context recall of 0.427 is the lowest among the answering
configurations. The hybrid configuration removes the identifier failure
and lifts context recall to 0.498, supporting the hybrid criterion (C2).

\paragraph{Generation mode.} V3 and V4 share the same retriever, so
their differences follow from the generation mode. The templated
pipeline raises answer relevancy by 0.169 and context recall by 0.112,
lifts the rubric total from 1.67 to 2.29, and reduces abstentions from
seven to zero; the higher context recall likely reflects this abstention
difference, since abstained queries leave ground-truth facts uncovered.
This comparison carries the main design contribution of the thesis: the
gains come from structuring the answer, not from changing the model or
the retrieval.

\paragraph{Local versus hosted generation.} Replacing the local 8B model
with the hosted model improves faithfulness by 0.159, answer correctness
by 0.043, and the rubric total from 2.29 to 2.39, with near-perfect
traceability (2.93). It also reduces
median generation latency from 85 to 6.4 seconds per query, a factor of
thirteen.
The price is the deployment model: hosted generation moves analyst
queries off premises and gives up the local-deployment property that C6
demands. The evaluated prototype therefore keeps the local model as its
primary configuration and treats hosted generation as an option whose
benefit is now quantified.

\section{Scenario-based evaluation of representative outputs}
\label{sec:representative-outputs}

This section moves beyond offline metrics (C4). Three instruments assess
the outputs from an analyst perspective: the four-dimension rubric of
Section~\ref{sec:ragas-metrics}, the field-coverage measurement, and a
walk-through of one representative output per task type. Full transcripts
are reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:outputs}, per-query details in
Appendix~\ref{app:additional-results}.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \small
    \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{tables/rubric_final_v1}}
    \caption{Analyst-oriented rubric scores (0--3 per dimension,
             three-run means). Rows correspond, top to bottom, to
             configurations V4, V3, V0, and V5 of

             V1 and V2 were not rubric-scored.}
    \label{tab:rubric-results}

The templated pipeline reaches a rubric total of 2.29, compared to 1.67
for the legacy prompt and 1.66 for the non-retrieval baseline. The
dimension profile of the baseline is the central finding of this table:
its actionability (1.87) exceeds the legacy configuration (1.10) and
almost matches the templated pipeline (1.77), while its traceability
collapses to 1.23. The baseline produces recommendations that sound
actionable but cannot be traced to evidence --- precisely the failure
mode that bottleneck B3 describes and criterion C1 is designed to
prevent. The hosted ablation leads every dimension (total 2.39,
traceability 2.93); Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation} weighs this
against its deployment cost.

Field coverage complements the rubric on the completeness side: across
the final runs, the templated pipeline covers 87--89\,\% of the
task-specific fields expected by the query set.

\subsection{Vulnerability analysis}

Query \texttt{vuln\_001} asks what CVE-2024-3094, the xz-utils
supply-chain backdoor, is and what its impact is.
Figure~\ref{fig:scenario-vuln} shows the workbench response to a live
query against the evaluated configuration; the status pills confirm the
templated pipeline and the local model.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{scenario_vuln_answer}
    \caption{Workbench response to query \texttt{vuln\_001} (live query,
             configuration V4). The deterministic head reports the triage
             signal with its rationale; every narrative claim carries a
             citation; the source panel separates cited from retrieved
             but uncited context.}
    \label{fig:scenario-vuln}
\end{figure}

Three properties of this response carry the assessment. First, the
deterministic head grades the finding as HIGH and states its rationale
--- CVSS 10.0, but no KEV listing and no ransomware evidence in the
corpus snapshot --- so the prioritization is not only given but
explained. Second, every narrative claim ends in a citation, and the
source panel distinguishes the two cited passages from the three
retrieved but uncited ones; the analyst sees not only what the answer
uses but also what it ignored (transparency, C1, C5). Third, the gaps
section reports the absence of indicators and CWE-specific mitigations
in the retrieved context instead of filling these slots from parametric
knowledge.

\textbf{Rationale:} CVSS 9.8 + active KEV listing + known ransomware use\\
\textbf{Vulnerability Facts:} CVSS 9.8; CWE-89; severity CRITICAL\\
\textbf{KEV Status:} listed 2023-06-02; known ransomware use: yes;
CISA required action: Apply updates per vendor instructions.\\[4pt]
\textbf{Exploitation Context}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The vulnerability CVE-2023-34362 allows an unauthenticated
          attacker to gain unauthorized access to MOVEit Transfer's
          database. \texttt{[Source: nvd\_CVE-2023-34362]}
    \item There is evidence of active exploitation in May and June 2023
          via HTTP or HTTPS. \texttt{[Source: nvd\_CVE-2023-34362]}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Gaps:} Indicators of Compromise (IoCs) for this specific

The answer passes seven of eight field-coverage checks. The
deterministic head answers the triage question at a glance

explicit statement of absence is the intended behavior of the grounding
design (transparency, C1).

The fulfilled criteria close the loop to the workflow bottlenecks of
Section~\ref{sec:cti}. The verification bottleneck (B3) is where the
prototype changes analyst work most clearly: enforced citations raise
traceability from 1.23 (baseline) to 2.56, and the interface separates
cited from uncited context, so verification becomes an inspection step
instead of a research task. The overload bottleneck (B1) is addressed
from two sides: hybrid retrieval keeps identifier queries reliable where
single strategies fail, and the templated output presents each answer in
a fixed triage structure. The recency bottleneck (B2) is addressed by
design --- the offline path can re-index updated sources at any time ---
but not demonstrated empirically, because the evaluation deliberately
ran on a frozen snapshot for reproducibility.

Two judgments deserve their qualification. C3 is fulfilled only
partially: ingestion is restricted to curated sources and every passage
keeps its provenance, but the prototype does not assess source
credibility at query time, as stated in Section~\ref{sec:requirements}.
For C5, the rubric confirms the presentation gains overall (2.29 versus
1.67), yet actionability is the weakest templated dimension (1.77): the
mitigation sections only relay what the corpus offers, and for many CVE
entries that is a single KEV remediation line or an explicit statement
of absence. Both boundaries carry over to the limitations in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

One scope note guards the C6 judgment: it refers to the evaluated
configurations V0--V4, which contain no hosted dependency. The hosted
model of the ablation (V5) is an evaluation instrument --- the measured
counterfactual of giving up local deployment --- and the evaluation
judge is measurement instrumentation, not part of the artifact. The
optional extended mode calls a hosted model and is documented as a
feature outside the evaluated configuration
(Section~\ref{sec:mcp-interface}).

\section{Reference model for CTI-RAG integration}
\label{sec:reference-model}

The empirical findings generalize beyond the prototype into a reference
model for integrating RAG into CTI and threat-hunting workflows
(Figure~\ref{fig:reference-model}). The model organizes an integration
into four layers inside a local deployment boundary --- curated sources,
a provenance-preserving knowledge layer, a layered reasoning stage, and
an analyst interaction layer --- flanked by an evaluation and governance
function. Eight design principles, marked P1--P8 in the figure, locate
the empirically grounded decisions; Table~\ref{tab:principles} states
each principle with the evidence it rests on.

\end{figure}

The model reads as adoption guidance: an organization integrating RAG
into CTI work starts at the source layer and moves upward, and every
principle names the failure it prevents --- P2 the identifier collapse
of semantic-only retrieval, P3 and P4 the unverifiable fluency of
ungrounded generation, P6 the silent loss of data control. The model is
derived from one prototype, one corpus, and one query set; transferring
it to other corpora and organizations requires validation, which
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} records and
Section~\ref{sec:future-work} takes up.

\section{Answering the research questions}
\label{sec:rq-answers}

The boxes below answer the research questions of
Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}; each answer names the evidence it
rests on.

\begin{rqbox}{MRQ}
    \emph{How can a RAG system leveraging LLMs improve the quality,
    reliability, and operational usefulness of CTI analysis and
    threat-hunting workflows?}\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Answer:} By grounding a locally deployed LLM in a curated,
    provenance-preserving corpus through hybrid retrieval and a layered,
    citation-enforcing generation pipeline. The improvement lies less in
    raw answer correctness than in verifiability and task fit: grounded
    answers can be checked claim by claim, abstain without evidence, and
    arrive in a triage-ready structure. The reference model consolidates
    these properties into transferable design principles.\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Evidence:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Correctness at baseline level, traceability transformed
              (1.23 to 2.56) --- Sections~\ref{sec:results-generation}
              and~\ref{sec:results-ablation},
              Table~\ref{tab:rubric-results}
        \item Five of six design criteria fulfilled, bottlenecks B1 and
              B3 addressed --- Section~\ref{sec:assessment}
        \item Reference model with eight principles ---
              Section~\ref{sec:reference-model}
    \end{itemize}
\end{rqbox}

\begin{rqbox}{SRQ1 --- Information retrieval and answer quality}
    \emph{To what extent does retrieval augmentation improve the
    completeness, factual accuracy, and recency of CTI information
    generated by an LLM compared to a non-retrieval baseline?}\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Answer:} Retrieval augmentation improves completeness and
    relevance, while factual accuracy on this query set stays comparable
    to the parametric baseline, which the prominent CVEs of the query
    set favor. The decisive gain is that retrieved facts are verifiable
    and current by construction: the corpus snapshot extends beyond any
    training cutoff, and claims without evidence are abstained from
    rather than generated.\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Evidence:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Answer relevancy 0.739 vs.\ 0.685, correctness 0.518 vs.\
              0.502, context recall 0.610 ---
              Table~\ref{tab:generation-results}
        \item Field coverage 87--89\,\% of expected task fields ---
              Section~\ref{sec:representative-outputs}
        \item Traceability 2.56 vs.\ 1.23; zero abstentions with explicit
              absence statements --- Table~\ref{tab:rubric-results},
              Section~\ref{sec:results-generation}
    \end{itemize}
\end{rqbox}

\begin{rqbox}{SRQ2 --- Practical utility and workflow support}
    \emph{Which types of CTI analysis tasks can be effectively supported
    by a RAG-based LLM system, and how well do the generated outputs
    align with the informational needs of threat-hunting workflows?}\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Answer:} All four task types of the query set are supported
    end to end. Support is strongest where deterministic structure
    carries the answer --- vulnerability analysis and cross-source
    comparison --- and remains consistent on TTP correlation, where the
    unstructured prompt becomes unstable. The main boundary is
    actionability: mitigation guidance can only relay what the corpus
    documents.\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Evidence:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Vulnerability analysis: triage signal with rationale,
              cited evidence --- Section~\ref{sec:scenario-vuln}
        \item IOC enrichment: seven of eight expected fields, absence
              stated instead of fabricated ---
              Section~\ref{sec:scenario-ioc}
        \item TTP correlation: five advisories consolidated; templated
              pipeline consistent where legacy is unstable ---
              Section~\ref{sec:scenario-ttp}
        \item Cross-source comparison: deterministic comparison table,
              source disagreements made explicit ---
              Section~\ref{sec:scenario-cross}
        \item Rubric total 2.29 vs.\ 1.67 (legacy) and 1.66 (baseline);
              actionability weakest at 1.77 ---
              Table~\ref{tab:rubric-results}
    \end{itemize}
\end{rqbox}

\begin{rqbox}{SRQ3 --- Conceptual design}
    \emph{How can empirical insights from the RAG-LLM prototype be
    translated into actionable design principles for integrating
    generative AI into CTI and threat-hunting workflows?}\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Answer:} The evaluation results condense into eight design
    principles --- from curated, provenance-preserving ingestion through
    identifier-aware hybrid retrieval and layered, citation-enforcing
    generation to local-first deployment with hosted generation as a
    quantified opt-in --- arranged in a four-layer reference model with
    an evaluation and governance function. Each principle names the
    empirically observed failure it prevents.\\[4pt]
    \textbf{Evidence:}
